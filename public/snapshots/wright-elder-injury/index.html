Bottom line

The legal credibility is easier to see than the digital structure.

The site makes the attorney experience and elder injury focus visible. It also lists concrete practice areas that families may be searching for. The gap is that the site does not package those facts clearly enough for search and AI systems to understand the firm as a local legal-services entity.

The opportunity is not to make a bigger website. It is to make the current one more explicit: who the firm is, where it serves, what matters most, and what a family should do next.

What is already working

This is a credible foundation.

The reviewed pages were reachable and included meaningful content around elder injury, nursing home negligence, assisted living negligence, medical malpractice, wrongful death, and related serious injury matters.

Strength

Clear attorney story

The About page names H. Micheal Wright and Lincoln M. Wright and explains deep experience in personal injury and elder injury matters.

Strength

Specific injury scenarios

The services page names concrete concerns including bedsores, falls, infection, medication errors, dehydration, and care-facility negligence.

Strength

Visible first step

The site offers a free consultation, shows a phone number on the homepage, and provides a contact form for people who need help.

Where it gets fuzzy

Important signals are present, but not packaged cleanly.

Families may understand the site after reading it. Search and AI systems need the same facts stated in more structured, consistent ways.

Signal gap

No structured data was detected

The scan did not find JSON-LD on the reviewed pages. A clean LegalService or LocalBusiness entity connected to attorney and service information would make the firm easier to interpret.

Local gap

Location is not easy to verify

The site references Arizona credentials and case history, but the reviewed pages did not make city, address, or service area as clear as they should be.

Content gap

Practice areas are listed more than explained

The services list is useful, but the highest-value topics need more answer-ready context: warning signs, timelines, evidence, next steps, and what families should expect.

Trust gap

Proof signals could be easier to corroborate

The site describes experience and specialization, but credential, review, profile, and case-history signals should be easier to verify from the page structure.

Proposed sprint

Wright Visibility Cleanup Sprint

A compact sprint would focus on the highest-leverage cleanup: local identity, structured data, practice-area clarity, and a more durable contact path. The point is to make the existing credibility easier to find, understand, and act on.

1

Confirm the canonical firm facts

Verify the preferred business name, address, city, phone, attorneys, service area, and primary practice categories before publishing structured data.

2

Add a clean legal-services schema layer

Represent the firm, website, attorneys, core services, contact action, and service area in JSON-LD that matches what visitors can verify on the page.

3

Tighten the highest-value practice areas

Turn the services list into clearer sections or pages for elder injury, nursing home negligence, medical malpractice, wrongful death, and serious injury matters.

4

Make contact and trust signals consistent

Keep the phone, form, attorney credentials, bar-specialization references, and next step visible in the places families naturally look for them.

5

Hand over a before/after note

Close the sprint with a short explanation of what changed, why it matters, and what the firm can build on next.

Suggested scope

Keep this focused.

The cleanest version is one practical cleanup pass. That keeps the work useful, bounded, and easy to evaluate.

Included

  • Canonical business facts and contact-path cleanup recommendations.
  • Structured data plan for the firm, attorneys, and core legal services.
  • Practice-area content hierarchy for the highest-value injury topics.
  • Trust-signal checklist for credentials, third-party profiles, and reviews.
  • Short before/after summary for Chuck and the family.

Boundaries

  • A full website redesign.
  • Ranking, traffic, lead, or AI-answer placement guarantees.
  • Legal claim review beyond page-structure and visibility clarity.
  • Unsupported schema claims that are not visible on the site.
  • Ongoing SEO campaign work unless the firm asks for it later.
